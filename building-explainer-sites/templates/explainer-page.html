Money doubles faster than most people guess

Divide 72 by the interest rate to get the doubling time

At a steady yearly return, money grows by a fixed percentage each year. Because each year's growth is added to the balance, the next year's growth is a little bigger. That is compounding.

A handy shortcut, the rule of 72, says the number of years to double is about 72 divided by the rate. At 6%, money doubles in about 12 years; at 9%, about 8.

Small rate differences become large balance differences

Going from 4% to 8% doesn't just double the growth; it halves the doubling time, from about 18 years to about 9. Over 36 years, that is two doublings versus four: four times versus sixteen times the starting amount.

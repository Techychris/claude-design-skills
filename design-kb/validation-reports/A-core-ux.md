## Missing or misframed

1. **Miller.** KB handles the 7 vs 4 myth well. Add that Miller's paper covered two separate limits (absolute judgment about 4 to 8 alternatives, span about 7) and that span varies by material (about 7 digits, 6 letters, 5 words) per Wikipedia. Add Cowan's population range 3 to 5, individuals 2 to 6.
2. **Hick's law misuse.** KB flags "not a search law" but never says what that implies: fewer menu items is not automatically faster. Add that random-list scanning is linear and that the law covers equally probable choices with little practice. I did not open menu-selection studies (e.g. Landauer and Nachbar) so cite none until read.
3. **Choice overload** is absent. Scheibehenne et al. 2010 meta-analysis (50 experiments, 5,036 participants) found the mean choice-overload effect "virtually zero"; more options helped most for food choices with clear preferences. Useful counterweight to "fewer options" folklore. Source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/01/100119121425.htm (press summary; paper not opened).
4. **Fitts.** Missing the practical rules that survive (edges and corners act as large targets for a mouse; cluster related controls) and the warning that touch is not well modelled by the mouse constants.
5. **F-pattern.** Framing is good. Missing: NN/g's own wording ("rough, general shape"; E and inverted-L variants). The 2017 participant counts are unsourced (#2).
6. **Doherty and response times.** Add that the 0.1/1/10 s limits trace to Miller 1968 and Card 1991 (Nielsen 1993), and that the Doherty figure is an IBM productivity claim. Do not present 400 ms as an attention law.
7. **Peak-end.** Reframe Kemp 2008 and Miron-Shatz 2009 as boundary-condition tests, not replications of the 1993 study.
8. **Touch target units.** A single consolidated table with unit and level (WCAG 24 CSS px AA / 44 CSS px AAA; Apple 44 pt default, 28 pt min; Material 48 dp plus 8 dp) is missing; the current text mixes them (#60).
9. **Color blindness.** Prevalence figures are Northern-European; global rates are lower (#38). Also Apple's "blue-orange" caution in addition to red-green.
10. **Dated items to refresh.** WebAIM screen reader survey (#40), DOJ date caveat (#49), FTC comment date (#50).
11. **Not checked, flag for a second pass (no source opened):** Gestalt claims, Jakob's law, Tesler's law, cognitive-load models (Sweller 1998, 2019, 2021), Norman's six principles, Kahneman-adjacent effects not in the KB, Material elevation (six levels), M3 type-scale values (page did not render), Apple HIG items in "Platform docs audit" other than those listed, Leiva/Burmistrov/Djamasbi eye-tracking studies, Baymard checkout stats, Spotify bottom-nav +9% and +30%, StatCounter share, Lindgaard 50 ms study.
12. **Principles absent from the KB that are widely cited:** aesthetic-usability effect (Kurosu and Kashimura 1995; not checked), serial position and Von Restorff, Law of Pragnanz, goal-gradient effect, paradox of the active user, Weber's law. All unverified by me; add only with sources.
